Module 1: Introduction to CIT301
Alright, so let's talk about CIT301. Basically, this course is about computers and how they work — but not in that boring textbook way. Think about it like this: you know how you use your phone every day? You chat on WhatsApp, you check your bank app, you watch TikTok — all of that is possible because of what you'll learn in CIT301.

In this module, we're going to break down what a computer really is. Forget the big definition in the PDF that says "an electronic device that processes data" — let's keep it simple. A computer is just a tool that takes what you give it, works on it, and gives you something useful back. Like when you type a message and it sends it. That's it.
• What is Computer?
So what exactly is a computer? In plain language, it's any device that can store your stuff, process it, and give you results. Your phone is a computer. Your laptop is a computer. Even that POS machine where you withdraw money — that's a computer too. The key thing is: you give it input (like typing), it processes (thinks), and gives output (shows result).

Module 1: Introduction to Computers

Okay, so let's be real. You've been using computers since you were small, but do you actually know what makes a computer a computer? In this module, we'll break it down without all that heavy textbook grammar.

A computer, in the simplest way, is like your personal assistant. You tell it "save this photo" and it saves. You tell it "calculate my GP" and it calculates. It's not magic — it's just a machine that follows instructions very fast.

• What is a Computer?
Forget the long definition from page 2 of your PDF. Let's keep it plain: A computer is an electronic device that takes information (like your name, your photos, your assignment), works on it, and gives you back something useful (like a printed document or a sent email). Your phone does this, your laptop does this. Even the ATM does this. That's a computer.

• Types of Computer
You know how we have different types of phones — iPhone, Samsung, small, big? Computers are similar. We have supercomputers (very powerful, used by big companies), mainframe (used in banks), personal computers (your laptop), and microcomputers (your phone). But honestly, for your exam, just remember: they differ by size, power, and where they are used. Your phone is actually more powerful than the computers that sent people to the moon in 1969. Crazy, right?
